## 3. Findings

**F1 — high. Shaping is not a stage, so the plan stalls while the loop stays busy.**
- **Claim:** `ready` lists only shaped issues, and nothing prompts anyone to shape the next plan
  slice. Capacity therefore goes to whatever the review roles have just shaped.
- **Evidence (measured):**
  - #379's timeline: labelled `risk:1` on 2026-09-23, then renamed and given `agent-ready` at
    2026-10-22T09:13Z. Its only blocker, #374, closed 2026-10-15. 20 + 4 + 52 = 76 merges landed in
    between (brief §2).
  - R878 (05:35Z): with nothing pickable, the coordinator shaped its verifiers' three `risk:1`
    follow-ups, not N.4, which is also `risk:1`.
  - At 09:13Z, `ready` offered 11 pickable tasks: #257 and ten `inbox-review` issues.
  - `.claude/skills/ha-next/SKILL.md:25-26`: if nothing is pickable, "say what would unblock the
    closest one … and stop". No step says to shape the plan's next slice.
- **Cost:** the foundation plan moves only when a coordinator remembers to shape it, and a month
  away advances process and parity, not the read-only pipeline.
- **Fix:** in `backlog.logic.mjs`, a `foundation` or `spine` slot with nothing pickable names the
  epic's oldest unblocked, unshaped sub-issue as `shape: #n`. The queue-up rule puts plan slices
  ahead of follow-ups. **Effort:** S–M. **Decides:** the coordinator, through the harness inbox.
- **Strongest case against:** a nudge cannot do the shaping itself; #379's body records design
  calls (elkjs rejected, synchronous layout). The coordinator shaped N.0–N.3 promptly once it
  turned to them, and the 10-21 run hardened the grading path that Project 1 runs on.

**F2 — high. The pick rule's first step is the one class of issue an agent may never pick.**
- **Claim:** an oracle divergence, the defect the product most promises to prevent, is filed
  `sev:critical` and `bot-filed`, so it waits for the owner. Yet `sev:critical` is the pick rule's
  first step.
- **Evidence (measured):**
  - `docs/portfolio.md`, pick rule 1.
  - `docs/harness/fidelity-brief.md:73-77`: "never `agent-ready` without the owner".
  - `docs/harness/issue-form.md:36`.
  - #907 was filed at 03:24Z and still waits. #906, from the same panel, was filed at 03:20Z and
    merged as #908 at 03:55Z.
- **Cost:** while the owner is away, every divergence parks, and the bugs reached first are the
  minor ones.
- **Fix:** a fidelity-filed divergence becomes `agent-ready` once a fresh verifier reruns its
  executable repro, or once the inbox panel (R844) approves it. `bot-filed` stays for content
  from outside. **Effort:** S. **Decides:** the owner, because this is the issue form's security
  boundary.
- **Strongest case against:** `bot-filed` stops the loop from granting itself work that jumps the
  whole cycle, so a self-filed critical is where a human gate costs least per unit of risk. #907
  is a false pass on one looping-wire shape, and it corrupts no data.

- **Cost (inferred):** about 455k tokens per approved one-line harness fix: the mean panel is 144k,
  the mean builder 164k (6.71M over 41) and the mean verifier 147k (5.44M over 37). Capacity
  converges on polishing what was already built.
